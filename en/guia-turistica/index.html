Pomaire Tourist Guide 2026 — Everything for your visit to the pottery village

Last updated: August 2026 | Reading time: 12 minutes

Pomaire is Chile's most famous pottery village and one of the most visited tourist destinations in the Metropolitan Region. Located just 50 minutes from Santiago via the Autopista del Sol (Route 78), this picturesque village in the Melipilla commune attracts more than 500,000 visitors per year thanks to its pottery tradition spanning over 300 years, its traditional cuisine, and the charm of its streets filled with artisan workshops.

In this complete tourist guide, updated for 2026, you'll find all the information you need to plan your perfect visit to Pomaire.

How to get to Pomaire from Santiago

By car

The most comfortable way to reach Pomaire is by car via the Autopista del Sol (Route 78). Take the exit at km 51 following signs to Pomaire. The journey from central Santiago takes approximately 50 minutes without traffic (can increase to 1 hour and 15 minutes on weekends during peak departure times, between 9:00 and 11:00 AM).

Tip: If you're going on a weekend, leave before 9:00 AM to avoid traffic jams on the highway and find parking easily.

By public transport

From Santiago you can take an intercity bus at the Terminal San Borja (Metro Estacion Central) heading to Melipilla. The fare is approximately $2,500 CLP. From Melipilla, take a shared taxi or local bus to Pomaire (15 minutes, ~$800 CLP). Buses run every 20 minutes.

There are also direct buses from Melipilla that pass through Pomaire. Ask the driver if the route includes a stop in the village.

On an organized tour

Several tourism agencies in Santiago offer half-day tours to Pomaire that include transportation, a guide and free time for shopping. Prices range from $25,000 to $45,000 CLP per person.

What to do in Pomaire — Must-do activities

  1. Visit the pottery workshops: There are over 50 workshops where you can watch artisans work the clay live. Many offer beginner classes.
  2. Have lunch at a traditional restaurant: Try the giant wood-fired oven empanadas, beef cazuela stew, and pastel de choclo (corn pie) served in clay pots.
  3. Visit the World's Largest Piggy Bank: A 2-meter clay sculpture created by master potter Cesar Silva Rojo.
  4. Buy handcrafted pottery: From small piggy banks ($2,000 CLP) to complete dinnerware sets and designer decorative pieces.
  5. Stroll through the Plaza de Pomaire: A meeting point featuring the San Antonio de Padua Church, artisan fair and family-friendly atmosphere.
  6. Taste chicha and artisan wine: Several shops offer traditional grape or apple chicha (fermented fruit drink) made the old-fashioned way.
  7. Explore the Melipilla Wine Route: Just a few kilometers away, boutique wineries offer tastings and vineyard tours.

Where to eat in Pomaire — Restaurants and traditional dishes

Pomaire is a gastronomic destination par excellence. The village has more than 20 restaurants serving traditional Chilean cuisine with a unique touch: everything is prepared and served in clay pots and plates made by the village's own artisans.

Dishes you must try

  • Wood-fired oven empanada: The most famous in Chile. Giant size, crispy dough and generous pino filling (beef, onion, egg, olive).
  • Beef cazuela: Served in a steaming clay pot with potato, squash, corn on the cob and beef.
  • Pastel de choclo (corn pie): In an individual clay dish, with pino filling, chicken, olive and sugar on top.
  • Humitas (corn tamales): Made with fresh corn, prepared in a homestyle fashion.
  • Artisan chicha: Grape or apple, traditionally fermented.
Food tip: Restaurants fill up between 1:00 PM and 2:30 PM on weekends. If you want to avoid waiting, have lunch before 12:30 PM or after 3:00 PM.

Pottery workshops — Handcrafted clay

Pottery is the heart of Pomaire. Over 50 workshops open to the public where artisans of up to 5 generations work the greda (local red clay) using ancestral techniques: hand modeling, wheel throwing, and firing in wood-burning kilns.

Available experiences

  • Free visit: Walk into any workshop and observe the creative process. Free of charge.
  • Pottery class: Several workshops offer 1-2 hour experiences where you create your own piece ($10,000 - $20,000 CLP).
  • Direct purchase: Buying at the workshop saves you the retail markup and directly supports the artisan.

Most sought-after products

  • Piggy bank (from $2,000 CLP)
  • Clay cooking pot ($8,000 - $25,000 CLP)
  • Artisan dinnerware set ($30,000 - $80,000 CLP)
  • Designer decorative pieces ($15,000 - $150,000 CLP)
  • Planters and garden pieces ($5,000 - $40,000 CLP)

Parking and getting around

Pomaire is a small village and easily explored on foot. The main street is about 800 meters long, and that's where all the workshops, restaurants and shops are concentrated.

Where to park

  • Calle Rafael Morande: Free street parking on the main road (limited on weekends).
  • North entrance: Large lot with space for buses and cars.
  • Private parking: Some restaurants offer parking to their customers.
Tip: On weekends and holidays, parking fills up before 11:00 AM. If you arrive later, look for spaces on the side streets south of the village.

Where to stay in Pomaire

If you want to enjoy Pomaire at a leisurely pace, staying overnight is an excellent option. There are hostels, cabins and rural suites with prices starting at $35,000 CLP per night.

  • Village hostels: Simple and affordable options within the tourist area.
  • Family cabins: Ideal for groups, with equipped kitchen and countryside views.
  • Rural suites: For couples, with rustic decor and breakfast included.

Best time to visit Pomaire

Pomaire can be visited year-round, but there are differences depending on the season:

  • Spring (Sep-Nov): Pleasant weather, fewer visitors than summer. Ideal.
  • Summer (Dec-Feb): Intense heat but peak atmosphere. All shops open.
  • Autumn (Mar-May): Mild temperatures, excellent for walking. Golden landscapes.
  • Winter (Jun-Aug): Cooler with occasional rain, but fewer tourists and lower prices.
Best day: If you prefer tranquility, visit between Tuesday and Friday. Weekends have more atmosphere but more crowds. Some shops close on Mondays.

Budget and prices 2026

Pomaire is a destination accessible for all budgets:

  • Full lunch: $8,000 - $15,000 CLP per person
  • Individual empanada: $3,500 - $5,000 CLP
  • Souvenir pottery: $2,000 - $15,000 CLP
  • Pottery class: $10,000 - $20,000 CLP
  • Round-trip bus fare: ~$5,000 CLP
  • Highway toll (car): ~$3,800 CLP one way
  • Estimated total budget: $20,000 - $50,000 CLP per person (half-day trip)

Tips and recommendations for visiting Pomaire

  1. Bring cash: Many small workshops and shops only accept cash. Larger restaurants do have card terminals.
  2. Wear comfortable shoes: The streets are uneven and most of the visit is on foot.
  3. Sun protection: In summer the sun is intense. Bring a hat, sunscreen and water.
  4. Buy at the end: Walk around first, compare prices and quality, then buy on your way back. Clay is fragile — better not to carry it the entire trip.
  5. Ask about shipping: Several workshops ship to Santiago if you buy large or fragile pieces.
  6. Arrive early: Especially on weekends. By 10:00 AM everything is open and there's still parking available.
  7. Respect the artisans: Ask permission before filming or photographing inside the workshops.
  8. Check the weather: In winter it can rain. Bring an umbrella and warm clothing.

What to visit near Pomaire

  • Melipilla Wine Route: Boutique wineries with tastings and paired lunches (15 min away).
  • Los Chinihues: A nearby rural locality with dryland landscapes and country life.
  • Melipilla: The main town of the commune with a market, shops and a historic plaza (15 min).
  • Lake Rapel: For water sports and river beach activities (45 min from Pomaire).
  • Vina Undurraga: Historic winery in Talagante with guided tours (30 min).
