Where to Eat in Pomaire: Giant Empanadas, Restaurants and Prices 2026

Last updated: September 2026 | Reading time: 8 minutes

Pomaire is one of the great food destinations in central Chile. Just 50 minutes from Santiago, this pottery village is famous throughout the country for its giant empanada and for a hearty country cuisine that is cooked and served in clay pots and plates made by the village's own artisans. In this guide we tell you what to eat, where and at what prices in 2026.

The giant empanada of Pomaire

The giant baked empanada from the clay oven is the star dish of Pomaire and one of the reasons thousands of people visit the village every weekend. In 1995, Pomaire made the news for making the largest empanada in the world, a milestone that stayed in the collective memory and cemented the village's culinary fame.

The giant version served today by several restaurants weighs close to one kilo and is filled with the classic Chilean pino: beef, onion, hard-boiled egg, olive and raisins. It is a generous portion meant to be shared or for the very hungry. Its price is around $6,000 to $7,000 CLP depending on the restaurant and the filling.

Tip: The giant empanada is huge. If two of you also want to try a cazuela or a pastel de choclo, a single empanada to share is enough.

Traditional dishes you must try

  • Baked clay-oven empanada: The most famous in Chile. Crispy dough and a generous pino filling. Available in regular and giant size.
  • Beef cazuela: Served in a steaming clay pot with potato, squash, corn and a good piece of meat. A comforting classic.
  • Pastel de choclo: In an individual clay dish, with pino, a piece of chicken, olive and toasted sugar on top.
  • Humitas: Made from fresh corn, prepared home-style, ideal in the summer season.
  • Arrollado huaso and pork cuts: Hearty country cuisine for those looking for something filling.
  • Artisanal chicha: Made from grapes or apples, fermented the traditional way. The village's typical drink.

How to choose a restaurant in Pomaire

In Pomaire there are more than 20 restaurants, most concentrated on the main street (Roberto Bravo / San Antonio) and around it. Almost all offer a very similar menu of traditional Chilean cuisine, so the choice usually depends on the atmosphere, the speed and how busy it is:

  • Traditional restaurants on the main street: The best known and most visited, with the giant empanada and a full menu. They fill up first on weekends.
  • Family-run spots and small eateries: More affordable and authentic, often run by the same family. Good value for money.
  • Restaurants with a patio or terrace: Ideal for going with children or in large groups, with more space.
Local tip: Look at where the pomairinos themselves and the regulars eat. A place full of locals is usually a better sign than one full only of passing tourists.

Opening hours and the best times to eat

Most restaurants open from Tuesday to Sunday, roughly between 11:00 and 18:00–19:00. On Mondays some places close or reduce their hours.

The busiest point is the weekend lunch between 13:00 and 14:30. If you want to avoid waits and find a table calmly, have lunch before 12:30 or after 15:00.

Tips for eating in Pomaire

  1. Bring cash: The large restaurants accept cards, but many small places only take cash.
  2. Share the giant empanada: It is huge; ideal for two people if you are also going to order another dish.
  3. Arrive early on weekends: From 13:00 everything fills up. Before 12:30 you will find a table without a problem.
  4. Ask for the dish of the day: It is usually the freshest and best-priced option.
  5. Leave room for dessert: Try the sopaipillas pasadas or the homemade rice pudding.

Frequently asked questions about where to eat in Pomaire

How much does the giant empanada of Pomaire cost?

The giant empanada, which weighs close to one kilo, costs approximately between $6,000 and $7,000 CLP depending on the restaurant and the filling. A regular baked empanada costs between $3,500 and $5,000 CLP.

Do restaurants in Pomaire accept cards?

Most large restaurants accept cards and have a POS terminal, but many small places only take cash. It is best to bring cash just in case.

Are there vegetarian options in Pomaire?

Yes, although the cuisine is mainly meat-based. You can find cheese empanadas, pastel de choclo (ask for it without meat), humitas and salads. It is best to ask at the venue.
